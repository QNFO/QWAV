## 0. Executive Summary

QWAV replaces the continuous geometry assumption underlying quantum computing and artificial intelligence with ultrametric (tree-based) geometry. The Bruhat-Tits tree from $p$ -adic number theory provides passive error suppression — zero logical errors at depth 7, 48$\times$ error reduction at zero additional qubit cost — through geometric structure alone, with no active quantum error correction. The same tree geometry enables glass-box AI: traceable decision paths through tree structure, explainable by design.

The program has produced 5 peer-accessible publications on Zenodo with registered DOIs, completed 2 spinoff specifications (Q-PNA v2.0 neural architecture, Convergence-Consilience hierarchical ontology), and submitted 7 applications to merit-based funding programs. Six applications remain pending. Revenue: $0. Team: solo founder.

The path forward is defined by 12 non-negotiable constraints — no physical lab, no peer review, written-only communication, solo founder, substance-first evaluation, no external dependencies — that are strategic filters, not limitations. The program operates inbound-only: Zenodo $\to$ ResearchGate $\to$ social distribution. No cold outreach. No groveling. Publish. Let the work speak.

The primary strategic uncertainty is the outcome of 6 pending applications. This document provides a theory of change, scenario plan, signal/noise taxonomy, risk register, and forward milestones that give the program a spine regardless of which scenario materializes.

### 1.2 Computational Evidence

- **Zero logical errors at depth 7** for physical error rates up to 40% on ternary ($p=3$) Bruhat-Tits trees
- **48$\times$ logical error reduction** via $q$ -ary scatter at zero additional qubit cost
- **Concatenation redundancy** — adding active QEC (surface code, Steane code) produces zero benefit; the tree already provides geometric suppression that standard QEC cannot improve upon
- **40-atom neutral atom hardware specification** — ternary tree of depth 3, Rydberg blockade gates, 4 K operation. Within demonstrated experimental capabilities.
- **Classical baseline** — honest comparative benchmarking: tree outperforms under correlated noise, loses under i.i.d. noise. This honesty is the credibility differentiator.

## 3. How We Get There — Theory of Change

### 3.1 The Causal Chain

```
1. Publish on Zenodo (DOI)
   ↓
2. Cross-post to ResearchGate + QNFO.org
   ↓
3. Distribute via social (Mastodon, Bluesky, Twitter/X)
   ↓
4. Right person sees the post or discovers the paper
   ↓
5. They read the work
   ↓
6. They are convinced by the substance
   ↓
7. They reach out (inbound)
   ↓
8. Conversation → collaboration, funding, or adoption
```

### 3.2 Assumption Audit

Each link in the chain has assumptions. Some are testable; some are not.

| Link | Assumption | Testable? | Confidence |
|:-----|:-----------|:----------|:-----------|
| 1 $\to$ 2 | ResearchGate indexing of Zenodo DOIs is reliable and timely | Yes — check ResearchGate for each DOI | HIGH |
| 2 $\to$ 3 | Social posts reach audiences that include potential readers (physicists, computer scientists, funders) | Partially — Buffer provides send confirmation but not reach analytics | MEDIUM |
| 3 $\to$ 4 | The right people are on Mastodon/Bluesky/Twitter and see QWAV content in their feeds | No — no engagement data, no follower analytics, no impression metrics | LOW |
| 4 $\to$ 5 | Seeing a post leads to reading a 30-50 page computational physics paper | No — bounce rate unknown | LOW |
| 5 $\to$ 6 | Reading the paper convinces a qualified reader that the thesis is sound | Partially — the computational evidence is strong, but the "assumptions gap" (L17) means mathematical proofs prove consistency, not physical reality | MEDIUM |
| 6 $\to$ 7 | A convinced reader acts — reaches out, cites, shares | No — action requires motivation beyond intellectual agreement | LOW |
| 7 $\to$ 8 | An inbound contact leads to a productive outcome | No — depends entirely on who reaches out and why | UNKNOWN |

### 3.3 Where the Chain is Weakest

The strongest link is 1 $\to$ 2 (publication infrastructure is reliable). The weakest link is **4 $\to$ 5 (reading): nobody reads papers.** This is not a hypothesis — it's measured. Zenodo analytics confirm near-zero readership across all 5 QWAV publications. The second-weakest link is 6 $\to$ 7 (conversion from readership to action), but this is downstream of the reading problem. Until someone reads the work, conversion is irrelevant.

### 3.4 Strengthening the Chain (Within Constraints)

| Weak Link | Strengthening Strategy | Constraint Conflict? |
|:----------|:----------------------|:---------------------|
| 4 $\to$ 5 (reading) | **BREAK THE CHAIN.** Do not ask people to read papers. Instead, produce interactive artifacts — simulations, visualizations, web tools — that demonstrate the thesis in 30 seconds. The interactive artifact IS the engagement. The paper is the archival backstop. | No — requires no live interaction, no external dependencies. Interactive artifacts are built by a single Projects agent in a single thread (D12). |
| 4 $\to$ 5 (reading) | Deploy interactive artifacts to GitHub Pages. Link from social media directly to the artifact, not to the paper. "Try this 30-second demo" replaces "Please read my 30-page paper." | No — written-only. The artifact speaks for itself. |
| 6 $\to$ 7 (action) | If the interactive artifact is compelling, the engaged user will find the formal DOI on their own. The artifact's page includes a prominent link to the Zenodo paper. | No |
| 3 $\to$ 4 (visibility) | Social media posts now link to an interactive experience rather than a PDF. This increases share probability — people share demos, not papers. | No |
| Long-term posterity | All artifacts remain accompanied by a Zenodo DOI with registered metadata. Researchers discovering the work years later via search queries will find the permanent DOI — the interactive artifact brings them in; the paper gives them something to cite. | No — this is the dual-channel strategy. |

## 4. What If It Fails — Scenario Planning

### 4.1 Scenario A: Best Case (Funded)

**Trigger:** FRO advances to full proposal OR Emergent Ventures awards grant OR EWOR accepts fellowship

**Posture:** 
- Funding unlocks: entity formation (revisit D12 SBIR block), dedicated computational resources, professional services
- Priority: deliver on funded commitments. All other program activity de-prioritized.
- Spinoff: any funded project becomes a dedicated spinoff under Projects agent

**Risk:** Funding creates obligations that may not align with the program's natural trajectory. Maintain constraint discipline — no live pitches even if funded.

### 4.2 Scenario B: Worst Case (All Rejections)

**Trigger:** All 6 pending applications return rejections + zero inbound contacts by September 2026

**Posture:**
- The program continues as-is: publish, distribute, wait. No structural change.
- The theory of change is re-evaluated: if the chain is broken at visibility (3 $\to$ 4), invest in bridge content and distribution. If the chain is broken at substance (5 $\to$ 6), the evidence is insufficient and needs strengthening.
- **This is not a failure.** The program's design — solo, low-cost, open-access, computational-only — means it can sustain indefinitely. "All rejections" is a continuation of the current state, not a collapse.

**Pivot trigger:** If 12 months pass with zero inbound contacts AND all applications rejected, the distribution strategy needs fundamental redesign. Options (within constraints): arXiv submission (conflicts with D2), targeted preprint sharing with specific researchers (conflicts with inbound-only), OR acceptance that QWAV operates in a pre-discovery phase indefinitely.

### 4.3 Scenario C: Most Likely (Deferred Interest)

**Trigger:** Mixed application outcomes (some rejections, some ongoing, no major funding). One or two low-signal inbound contacts that don't convert.

**Posture:**
- This is the baseline scenario. The program continues its rhythm: monitor applications, maintain publications, respond to inbound contacts case-by-case.
- The 6-month milestones (M1-M4) are designed for this scenario.
- If M5 (funding or institutional support) is not met by month 12, evaluate whether to formally shift to a self-sustaining publication trajectory that does not depend on external funding.

## 5. How We Know It's Working — Signal Detection

### 5.1 Signal Taxonomy

| Signal Type | Examples | Strength |
|:------------|:---------|:---------|
| **Direct engagement** | Inbound email from a researcher, citation in a paper, conference invitation, collaboration inquiry | STRONG — someone read the work and acted |
| **Platform engagement** | Paper views/downloads on Zenodo, ResearchGate reads, social post interactions (boosts, replies, quote-posts) | MODERATE — someone saw the work; doesn't guarantee they read or were convinced |
| **Institutional response** | Application acceptance, funding award, fellowship offer | STRONG — institutional validation of substance |
| **Network propagation** | Paper shared by someone with relevant credentials, discussed in a forum or mailing list QWAV doesn't control | STRONG — organic spread beyond the program's own distribution |
| **Replication/verification** | Independent computational reproduction of results, experimental test by a lab | STRONGEST — the gold standard of scientific validation |

### 5.2 Noise Taxonomy

| Noise Type | Examples | Why It's Not a Signal |
|:-----------|:---------|:----------------------|
| **Vanity metrics** | Follower counts, like counts, view counts without engagement | Indicates exposure, not conviction |
| **Bot/spam engagement** | Generic replies, automated follows, cryptocurrency pitch DMs | Zero information |
| **Self-generated activity** | Publishing another paper, making another post, submitting another application | Activity is not outcome. Distinguishing action from result is essential. |

| R4 | **Application exhaustion** — all 6 pending applications return rejections, and no new merit-based programs are identified | MEDIUM | LOW — the program costs nearly nothing to maintain | This is the baseline scenario (Scenario B). | R5 | **Founder burnout or priority shift** — the solo founder reallocates attention to other projects or life priorities | UNKNOWN | HIGH — the program has no redundancy | The low-cost, low-intensity design mitigates burnout risk. The program can idle for months without damage. The risk is not the founder stopping — it's the founder stopping AND no inbound contacts arriving during the idle period. |

## 7. What We Won't Do — Constraints as Strategy

These 12 decisions are documented in `DECISIONS.md`. They are reproduced here because they define the program's strategic posture — what it will not do is as important as what it will.

| # | Constraint | Strategic Rationale |
|:--|:-----------|:--------------------|
| D1 | No physical lab | Computational validation is faster, cheaper, and fully under program control. The evidence produced is real — computational physics is a legitimate field. |
| D2 | No peer review | The system is broken. Gatekeeping by credential is the problem QWAV is designed to route around. Open-access publication with registered DOIs provides permanence and citability without surrender to gatekeepers. |
| D3 | Written-only | Selects for audiences that evaluate substance over presentation. Eliminates the energy drain of live pitching. All communication is archivable, shareable, and improvable. |
| D4 | Solo founder | The program's speed comes from autonomy. Team-building introduces coordination overhead that would slow the publication rhythm without adding value at this stage. |
| D5 | Substance-first | Don't waste time on audiences that reject based on credentials. Target programs like Emergent Ventures, Foresight Institute, EWOR, and FRO that explicitly evaluate ideas on merit. |
| D12 | No external dependencies | Every task must be completable in a single LLM thread. External collaborators, APIs, labs, and multi-thread coordination introduce latency and risk incompatible with solo-founder speed. |
