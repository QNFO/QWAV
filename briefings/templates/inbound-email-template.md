# Inbound Email Response Template

**Purpose:** Standard response for substantive inbound contacts from people who read QWAV's published work. Not for cold outreach replies, spam, or automated messages.

**When to use:** A researcher, investor, or curious reader reaches out after engaging with QWAV content (publications, interactive artifacts, social posts).

**Tone:** Professional, confident, substance-focused. No groveling. No over-selling. The work speaks.

---

## Template

```
Subject: Re: [Original Subject]

[Name],

Thank you for reaching out. I appreciated [specific reference to what they mentioned -- their question, their comment, the specific publication/artifact they referenced].

[If they asked a technical question: answer it. If they expressed interest: point them to the most relevant artifact or publication.]

The full publication archive is on Zenodo (zenodo.org/communities/qwav/) and all code is open-source on GitHub (github.com/QNFO). Interactive demos are deployed at qnfo.github.io/QWAV/.

[If appropriate: I'd be interested in [discussing further / hearing your thoughts on X / etc.] -- but keep it low-pressure.]

Best,
Rowan

Rowan Brad Quni-Gudzinas

qwav.tech | github.com/QNFO | zenodo.org/communities/qwav/

```

---

## Rules

1. **Always reference what they specifically engaged with.** Generic responses signal that you didn't read their message.
2. **Always link to interactive artifacts, not just papers.** D13 compliance.
3. **Always link to Zenodo community and GitHub org.** Give them paths to explore.
4. **Never pitch.** Don't ask for funding, collaboration, or favors in a first response.
5. **Never over-claim.** Don't say "we're building the future of quantum computing." Let the work speak.
6. **Keep it under 150 words.** Brevity signals confidence.
7. **Respond within 48 hours.** Speed matters for inbound contacts.

## Variations

### For investor inquiries
Add: "QWAV is a solo research program. All publications and computational evidence are open-access. If your interest is investment-related, I'm happy to discuss -- the technical site and Zenodo archive provide the full evidence base."

### For researcher/collaboration inquiries
Add: "All methods and code are open-source and reproducible. The interactive demos at qnfo.github.io/QWAV/ demonstrate the key results. If there's a specific technical question, I'm happy to discuss."

### For general curiosity
Add: "The best starting point is the Ultrametric Quantum Computing Foundations paper (10.5281/zenodo.20154557) -- it's a 3,700-word accessible overview with falsifiable predictions. The interactive demos at qnfo.github.io/QWAV/ demonstrate the key results."
